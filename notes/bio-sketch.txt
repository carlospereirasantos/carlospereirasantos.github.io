driven real-time systems, applied AI, game engine technology, and distributed computing.
Alongside his technical work, he shapes the university's AI policy, infrastructure, and
laboratory strategy, and leads applied AI research initiatives.

Personal Statement
After 10 years in industry as Technical manager, I spend the last 15 as a researcher, educator, and team
builder working at the intersection of Artificial Intelligence, Game Technology, and interactive digital
experiences. My career has been guided by a consistent goal: to improve European competitiveness in Digital
Entertainment and Creative Industries by establishing practice-oriented connections between academia and
industry — finding research-based solutions to real problems, and educating professionals who make a
meaningful difference.
After a decade leading the Cradle research team, I developed a research identity that bridges industry
practice and academic rigour. My work spans embodied AI, serious games, virtual production, and extended
realities, always grounded in real-world deployment and societal relevance. None of this has been achieved
alone; every milestone reflects the collective effort of the researchers, students, and industry partners I have
had the privilege of working with.
I am seeking a part-time role at TU/e as a deliberate bridge between an applied and a research university. My
goal is to strengthen the connection between both institutions, anchoring joint research while bringing
applied implementation expertise, lab infrastructure, and industry networks. I aim to allocate my time as
follows: Research (30%), Education (20%), Impact (20%), Team & Leadership (30%).
My longer-term ambition is to contribute to a stronger, more innovation-driven European research ecosystem
— one where applied sciences universities and research universities work in genuine complementarity,
producing research that is both methodologically rigorous and relevant to industry and society. I am
committed to contributing to Europe's digital sovereignty, ensuring that creative, AI, and design industries
develop on European values of openness, responsibility, and human-centred innovation. This role also
represents a deliberate shift in my career focus: from building and leading large applied research teams,
toward deepening research quality and co-developing the institutional partnerships that will define the next
period of my academic contribution.
Ultimately, what motivates me most is the opportunity to inspire and equip the next generation — because I
firmly believe that the young researchers and students we work with today will be the ones solving
tomorrow's most important challenges.

Identity as an Educator
BUas operates within a Project-Based Learning model where students engage with real-
world challenges across integrative blocks. My role as an educator goes beyond teaching, it
involves designing the conditions in which meaningful learning can happen.
Education achievements
I have been involved in the design and development of several programmes within the
Academy for AI, Games and Media: the Bachelor in Applied Data Science and Artificial
Intelligence, the Master in Game Technology, and the forthcoming Master in Physical AI.
This work includes curriculum architecture, Intended Learning Outcomes definition, directly
linking European projects (VPSN, AVENUE, VPSN2) to educational programme , and the
development of lab facilities, including the AI Makerspace, XR Stage Lab, Motion Capture
Studio, and Photogrammetry Studio that connects research with practice. Alongside this, I
have built sustained relationships with industry partners, ensuring that the challenges
students work on are shaped by real professional contexts.
Vision of Education
I believe Europe needs stronger innovation pathways where practitioners with deep industry
knowledge can apply research methods to solve real problems. Universities of Applied
Sciences are uniquely positioned to build these pathways, but only if education and research
are genuinely mutually reinforcing: student projects feed into research questions, research
outputs feed back into educational design, and industry partners co-shape both.
The BUas-TU/e partnership is central to realising this vision at scale. By combining BUas's
applied, practice-oriented culture with TU/e's research rigour and doctoral infrastructure, we
can create learning environments where students benefit from both institutions' expertise —
and where supervision becomes a collaborative research act rather than an administrative
responsibility.

Identity as a Researcher & Vision of the Research Field
I am an applied researcher working at the intersection of Game Technology, Artificial Intelligence, and
human-computer interaction. My research asks how intelligent, interactive systems can be designed to
produce insight and impact in real-world contexts, from understanding human behaviour and
experiences, to AI-driven tools for complex societal decision-making.
I research I do has multiple audiences in mind: publications for academic communities, frameworks and
tools transferable to educational practice, and solutions directly usable by industry partners. This triple
orientation reflects my conviction that applied sciences research is the critical bridge between
fundamental inquiry and societal relevance.
Main Research Achievements (last 8 years)
Four key outputs demonstrate the breadth and applied impact of my research:
Procedural Ocean (2023–2024): Led Horizon Europe project generating AI-driven 3D offshore
environments based on GIS data for energy planning and decision-making. Published BUILD-IT 2023,
COG 2026.
PlanV (2021–2023): Virtual Productions applied research (w/ Painting Practice) using VR pre-
visualisation built on Unreal Engine, enabling film and television production professionals to iterate on
set design, cameras, lighting, and animation in real time, reducing production costs while increasing
efficiency. Conceived deployed on His Dark Materials (BBC/HBO) and Wednesday (Netflix).
VIBE (2018–2023): Led project developing photogrammetry-based digital human avatars and
embodied conversational agents for medical simulation training. Published ACM IVA 2023, ACM IUI
2023.
MSP Challenge (2016–2019): Co-developed multi-awarded serious game platform for Marine Spatial
Planning across five international seas. Published in Journal of Ocean Technology and Ecology &
Society.
Research Ambitions & Strategy (next 5–10 years)
My agenda continues to center on Human collaboration with Intelligent Systems, driven from the Game
Technologies and Digital Twins, and I will explore how Physical AI can be embodied, grounded in physical
environments, and support human decision-making.
The main driver, developing research on ethical by design AI, and the use of creative practices at the
intersection of AI, design, and societal impact.

Identity as a Value Creator & Vision of Impact
I see impact as the natural endpoint of applied research, the moment when a tool, framework,
or platform moves beyond the lab and changes how people work, decide, or learn. My impact
spans three domains: industry practice (tools deployed in professional film and healthcare),
policy and governance (serious game platforms used by marine planners and government
agencies), and education and society (AI literacy frameworks and Virtual Productions
Community adopted across Europe).
Main Achievements in Impact (last 8 years)
VP Gathering: Founded and organised annual Virtual Production industry-education
conference in Breda, for the last 6 years, and now bringing 250 participants bridging
education, research and creative industry practice at national and international level.
Development of advanced BUas Lab Facilities: Established and led development of the AI
Makerspace, XR Stage, XR Lab, Motion Capture Studio, and Photogrammetry Studio —
infrastructures available for education, research, and industry partners across BUas and the
Netherlands.
Educational Programme Support: Co-developed the Applied Data Science & AI (ADS&AI)
BSc programme at BUas, and contributed externally to the development of obstetric
simulation training through the VIBE project, and embedding research outcomes directly
into professional medical education.
Keynote presentations and Industry Partnerships: Established and maintains
communication and strategic partnerships with Epic Games, NVIDIA, and working now with
Google DeepMind, connecting BUas research and education to leading global technology
organisations.
KreativEU — leading the work across an alliance of 11 European universities, developing
shared frameworks for responsible and sustainable AI in creative industries and cultural
heritage contexts.
Ambitions & Strategy for Impact
My ambition is to establish BUas-TU/e collaboration focusing on applied AI impact in the
Brabant region and beyond. This means co-developing an industry network where research
questions are co-shaped by professional partners, outcomes are rapidly transferred into
practice, and open science principles guide dissemination. I will continue pursuing funding that
rewards societal relevance alongside academic rigour.

Team and Leader
Identity as a Leader & Vision of Academic Citizenship
My identity is shaped by two decades of experience across industry and academia: as a technical and project lead on critical safety software for the European Space
Agency, and as the founder and leader of multiple research teams at BUas. I understand leadership from both sides: what it means to be an effective team member who
contributes to shared goals, and what it takes to build a team from scratch and guide it to independence.
I want to be clear that none of my achievements are individual. Every project, programme, and platform I have been associated with is the result of collective effort —
and I am genuinely appreciative of every person I have had the pleasure of collaborating with. I believe good leadership is about people development, but the growth of
the individuals behind them, good outputs are a byproduct.
My role has been to create the conditions for that collaboration to thrive: designing the structure, recruiting the right people, establishing shared culture and working
agreements, and stepping back as teams like Cradle and the XR Stage became self-sustaining, and now I am doing the same with the AI Makerspace.
Leadership Development Ambitions
My leadership ambitions operate at three levels.
At team level, I aim to continue developing research leadership capacity within BUas, mentoring junior researchers into PI roles and supporting staff pursuing doctoral
training at TU/e.
At institutional level, I am working directly with the Academy Management Team and BUas Executive Board on AI strategy and research governance, and aim to
formalise the BUas-TU/e partnership into a sustainable collaborative structure with shared supervision and joint industry engagement.
At European level, through consortia, I will continue building leadership roles in research projects and alliances that advance responsible AI and practice-oriented
research across the continent.
